Emergency lighting monitoring

We commission monitoring systems for emergency and escape luminaires and carry out their testing and inspections. Emergency lighting has to work when the power fails – and during a fire safety inspection you have to prove it with up-to-date records.

Glamox · GFS · TM Tech

What the standard requires

The European standard EN 50172 describes how emergency lighting should be tested:

  • daily – a visual check that centrally supplied systems are ready,
  • monthly – a short simulated power failure to check that every luminaire lights up,
  • annually – a test for the full rated emergency duration of the luminaires.

Test results, faults, repairs and changes to the installation are recorded in a log book, either by hand or automatically by a monitoring system.

National fire safety regulations add their own requirements – such as inspection intervals and who may carry out the checks – and these differ from country to country. We set up testing to match the requirements that apply to your building.

What we do

  • configure and commission emergency luminaire monitoring systems – addressable self-test systems and central battery systems,
  • integrate emergency lighting monitoring with the DALI lighting control system, where the building has one,
  • set up schedules for automatic monthly and annual tests,
  • carry out periodic inspections and tests,
  • keep the test log book and prepare reports for inspections,
  • replace faulty luminaires, batteries and modules.

Why use a monitoring system

In a building with hundreds of luminaires, checking each one by hand every month takes a lot of time and it is easy to miss one. A monitoring system runs the tests itself, records the results and immediately shows which luminaire needs attention. The log book is always up to date, with no manual paperwork.

  • central battery systems

Frequently asked questions

How often does emergency lighting need to be tested?

Under EN 50172: a short functional test every month and a full-duration test once a year, plus a daily visual check for centrally supplied systems. National regulations may add further requirements, so we agree the exact schedule for each building.

Can the test log book be kept electronically?

Yes. The standard allows the log book to be kept by hand or as a printout from automatic test equipment. A monitoring system records the test results itself.

Can you take over a system that was serviced by someone else?

Yes. We start by reviewing the installation and its documentation, then take over testing, the log book and service.
