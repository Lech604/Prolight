04

Lighting system upgrades

We upgrade outdated lighting control systems and lighting installations. Modern controls use less energy and are easier to operate and service – and an upgrade typically pays for itself within 12–18 months.

Audit + design + delivery

When an upgrade makes sense

  • energy bills keep rising, and lights stay on where and when no one is there,
  • spare parts for the old system are no longer available, or no one will service it,
  • the room layout or the way the building is used has changed,
  • you can't check the state of the installation or change settings without a service visit,
  • emergency lighting is tested by hand and the log book is hard to keep up to date.

How an upgrade works

  1. Audit – we review the building and its existing systems and identify what can stay and what needs replacing. The first consultation is free.
  2. Design and quote – a detailed plan and clear terms, with no hidden costs.
  3. Delivery – to schedule and, if needed, in stages, so the building can keep operating.
  4. Service – after handover we stay available for support.

What you gain

  • lower energy consumption through presence detection, daylight harvesting, dimming and schedules,
  • zone and scene control that matches how the building is used today,
  • status information – the system reports failed luminaires itself,
  • automatic emergency lighting tests and an up-to-date log book, when integrated with the new system,
  • a system based on the open DALI standard that can be extended.

Frequently asked questions

Does the whole installation need replacing?

Not always. After the audit we show which luminaires and cabling can stay and which parts are worth replacing so that the upgrade pays off.

Will the upgrade stop the building from operating?

We plan the work to affect the building as little as possible – in stages if needed, zone by zone.

How quickly does an upgrade pay for itself?

Typically within 12–18 months. We prepare a more precise estimate after the audit, based on the condition of the installation and how the building is used.
